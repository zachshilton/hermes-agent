# Q4 and seasonal

Q4 is treated as a different game, not a busier version of the same one. This is
the only place in the corpus that covers it, and several rules here invert the
year-round advice.

## The gift-angle inversion — the standout rule

**For a niche product in Q4, do NOT target the niche's own audience. Target the
people buying a gift FOR someone in that niche.**

Angles: "the perfect gift for [niche]", "don't show [person] this".

His claim: a correctly gift-angled niche product **converts higher than a
problem-solving product** in Q4 — but only if you stop targeting the niche itself.

The reasoning: Q4 buyers with holiday money mostly spend it in person or on brands
they know (Amazon, Gucci, dinners, Ubers) rather than seeking out unknown online
stores. The conversion path that survives is *"I saw this and thought of someone"*,
not self-directed niche shopping.

**Note this is a quarter-specific exception** to the year-round framing that value
products convert best and that a winner needs wow + value + niche together.

## The timeline, with real dates

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **By 1 November** | Product **in hand** |
| **By the end of the first week of November** | Accounts **warmed up** |
| Trigger signal | When Costco / Sainsbury's start putting up Christmas displays, you should already be ready |

This is much earlier than the standard cycle. **Being early with stock sitting
around beats being late** — stated explicitly, and it overrides the usual "5 days
prep, ~9 day test" cadence for this window only. General planning horizon the rest
of the year stays "two to three weeks ahead, max".

## Seasonal products generally: 2-3 months ahead

Beyond Q4, the lead time for any seasonal or event product is **two to three
months**, not two to three weeks. Worked examples he gives:

- Solar-eclipse glasses: started **2 months out**
- Summer products: planned from **February/March**
- Halloween: planned from **August/September**

This sits alongside the Q4 dates rather than conflicting with them -- Q4 is the
same principle with a harder deadline, because the window is fixed and the volume
is larger.

## The market is cyclical on roughly a five-year loop
"Q1 of 2022 is going to be the same as Q1 of 2025, and Q4 of 2022 is going to be
the same as Q4 of 2025."

So the research method for a season is to look at **what worked in the same quarter
in previous years and re-run it**, rather than inventing something new.

## The biggest Q4 mistake
**Assuming you must run seasonal or Christmas-themed products.** Evergreen
problem-solving products work fine in Q4 and can outperform, purely on the volume
increase.

## Why the quarter is worth the trouble
He puts a niche's buyer pool at roughly **1 million in Q1-Q3 and 10-20 million in
Q4** — approximate, and his framing is "micro windows of arbitrage" rather than a
measured figure. That expansion is the whole argument for the seasonal push.

## Q4 defined
October 1 to December 31.

## One caution he raises himself
He explicitly warns against black-hat Q4 tactics — the example given is fake
"donate to our church" framing — as illegal and beneath the target. See
`legal-flags.md`; the UK position on misleading commercial practices is stricter
than the framing in these videos assumes.
