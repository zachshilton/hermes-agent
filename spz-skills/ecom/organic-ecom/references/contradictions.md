# Where the sources contradict each other

Found by extracting 15 videos and comparing them. These are real conflicts in the
material, not extraction errors. **SPZ must never silently pick a side.** Present
both, say which is newer where that is known, and let Zach decide.

## 1. CTA in the creative, or not

- **Newer AI-organic videos:** the five-step creative ENDS with a CTA clip
  (result shown, then "comment <keyword>").
- **The older 8h free course:** *"Don't put a CTA in your viral-attempt video."*
  Post for virality first, then reply to the top comments with the CTA or pin a
  separate one. A CTA embedded in the original **was observed to suppress reach**.

Irreconcilable as stated. Most likely evolution, since the AI videos are recent —
but he never acknowledges changing position. Worth Zach testing on his own
accounts, which is cheap: it is one variable.

## 2. Marketing claims vs his own base rates

The "copy and paste" series is built around a case study at **$42k-$51k per week**
(~$180k-$220k/month), sold as copy-and-paste.

The one deep-dive video that steps back says:
- *"You can't go viral every single time"* — the title is acknowledged as clickbait
- **"90% of organic drop shippers don't make money"**
- A realistic beginner "good month" takes **"five months of trying"**
- **"$10k a month is not very hard to hit"** is the *unremarkable baseline*, before
  the differentiating work starts

The two framings coexist across the channel and are never reconciled. **The second
set is the honest one** and is what SPZ should use when Zach asks what to expect.

## 3. Website effort

- 4h course: build the site properly BEFORE going viral, for your first 3-5 stores.
- A client interview: his biggest beginner mistake was **over-focusing on the
  website instead of the marketing**.

Reconcilable in practice: build it adequately and quickly, then spend your time on
content. Both agree a bad site wastes traffic; they disagree on where the hours go.

## 4. Niche targeting

- One video: pair a proven product ARCHETYPE with a proven NICHE.
- The flagship: *"You're not targeting a niche... you're targeting problems,
  insecurities."*

The wow/value/niche framework probably subsumes the first — niche is one of three
components, and niche-alone is explicitly called volatile. That reading is a
synthesis, not something he says.

## 5. Novelty

- *"New is always better... you have to be novel"* — about CREATIVES.
- *"New is not better, better is better"* — about BUSINESS MODELS.

Different objects. Novelty in creatives is required; novelty in business model is
shiny-object syndrome. State the distinction rather than the conflict.

## 6. The same study, two sets of numbers

His protein-flavour choice-overload study appears in both long courses:
- one gives ~40 flavours -> 77 buyers per 1,000, and 3 flavours -> ~270
- the other gives 20 flavours -> 100 buyers, and 3 flavours -> 4x higher conversion

Same study, incompatible figures, in his own two videos. **Do not quote a number
for it.** The direction — fewer choices convert better — is the only safe claim.

## 7. "Top 1% make 98%" vs "99%" vs "99.999999%"

Three figures for one claim across three videos. It is rhetoric, not data.

drift or an unacknowledged decline.
