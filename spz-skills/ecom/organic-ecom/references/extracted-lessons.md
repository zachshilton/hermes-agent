## Priming Your Mind (Intro, 11:33)

The model rests on three things, and they are built rather than learned:

- **Skill** — capability at each facet of the work: editing, speed, finding
  winning products, recording. Described as black and white, and not
  something you can talk your way around. The fastest route to money in any
  business is closing the knowledge gap as fast as possible.
- **Judgement** — the ability to identify a clear course of action. Built
  from reps, and from watching what worked and failed for people around you,
  not only your own attempts.
- **Experience** — comes only from doing. Named as the reason he is critical
  of people who do reps inconsistently, or quit after a week.

**The operative rule: shift focus from outcomes to efforts.** A product that
makes nothing is a rep, not a verdict on you. The analogy given is the gym —
you do not leave upset that you are not benching 300lbs; you are proud of the
effort and build it over time through reps and consistency.

**How to apply this when talking to Zach.** When a product or a batch of
posts does nothing, the course's own framing is that this is a completed rep,
and the question is what the next rep changes — not whether the thing was a
failure. Do not offer consolation; ask what the next rep is.

## Automation & Split Testing (AI Mastery, 19:22)

A creative is treated as a set of **variables**, and split testing means
changing one while holding the rest constant.

Variables *of* a creative: hook, spot, music, concept, model, controversy.

Variables to **keep constant**: lighting (natural and imperfect), video style
(phone-style shot), product (same product, same colour), background (same
lighting, same room; vary only furniture style).

The loop, in the course's numbering:

    4. Analyse posts and find outliers
    5. Analyse outliers and find the working variables
    6. Automate

Creatives are assembled from interchangeable clip banks — hook clips, bridge
clips, character clips, CTA clips — recombined so that no clip is reused in
the same arrangement:

    hook clip 1 + bridge clip 4 + context clip 3 + CTA clip 3 = creative 1
    hook clip 4 + bridge clip 1 + character 2 + CTA clip 3 = creative 2

The board carries its own warning, worth keeping verbatim in spirit: it will
never be as simple as asking an AI to "make me viral videos" — editing and
manual change are still required, and the point of finding the outlier
variables first is so that generated variants do not just re-use clips.

**How to apply this here.** This is the part of the course that maps most
directly onto CLZ. A caption or creative instruction given to CLZ should
change one named variable, so that the result is attributable. Note the hard
limit though: **nothing in the dashboard measures outliers** (see the blind
spot in `../SKILL.md`), so step 4 — analyse posts and find outliers — cannot
be done with the tools available. Zach has to supply which posts were
outliers before steps 5 and 6 mean anything.
