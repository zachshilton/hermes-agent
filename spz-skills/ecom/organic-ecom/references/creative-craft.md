# Creative craft — the physical details

Everything here is about how a creative is actually made, as distinct from what it
says. Several are the kind of thing that quietly caps a video's performance.

## Three purchase drivers — use this to pick an angle
Every creative angle should lead on ONE of:

1. **Convenience** — it makes something easier
2. **Shine** — the wow factor. Either a striking-looking product OR a problem
   solved in a satisfying way
3. **Problem** — it removes a real pain

If you cannot name which of the three your creative leads on, the angle is not
defined yet.

## Lighting
- **Natural or outdoor light beats a single ring light.** Indoors, use three-point
  lighting or two to three softboxes.
- The concept that matters is **colour temperature / warmth, not brightness**. A
  bare phone flashlight or LED panel has no warmth, which is why footage lit that
  way reads as cheap.
- Do not overexpose or over-warm in camera -- shoot neutral or slightly cool and
  add warmth in the edit.
- Keep the subject in focus rather than the background, and face the camera
  toward the light source.

## Background
- **Cap background elements at two to three. Or none.**
- Do not copy another creator's exact setup. Build background, sound, lighting,
  captions, landing page and copy around **your** product's ICP.
- **Avoid the dropshipper aesthetic** -- marble background, ring light, fake plant,
  fake candle. It signals "advert" and traps you in the loop where your engagement
  comes from other dropshippers rather than customers.

## Length and pacing
- Videos should be **exactly as long as they need to be**. Padding for watch time
  lowers retention percentage, which is the metric that matters.
- Long-form is not penalised on Instagram or TikTok -- see `growth-channels.md`
  for why (playback speed does not reduce counted watch time on those platforms,
  unlike YouTube). Reels cap at 90 seconds.

## Post Product Analysis (PPA)
**After a product fails, study why -- including studying other people who ran the
identical product.** The question to answer is whether it was the creative, the
angle, or the product itself. Without this the next test repeats the same mistake
with a different product.

## A tactic he says does NOT work
**Fake burner-account comments** ("where can I get this?") to manufacture apparent
demand. His verdict: it is fake signal passed between burner accounts with no real
buyers behind it.

Note this is distinct from the phantom-account rage-bait tactic described
elsewhere in the corpus, which is about farming engagement after real virality --
and which is a platform-terms breach in any case (see `legal-flags.md`).

## Underfunded paid testing does not test anything
A mentee spent **$20 total across 4 ad sets and 20 creatives** -- some got zero
impressions. If you are going to test on paid at all, fund it properly or stay
organic. Related figures elsewhere in the corpus: under $1,000 of ad spend, most
people will not succeed "unless you get lucky".

## Minimum run before killing a product
**At least two weeks**, stated twice. This corroborates the 1.5-2 week testing
window in `accounts-and-testing.md` from an independent video.
