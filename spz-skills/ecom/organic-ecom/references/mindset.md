# Working psychology

Zach asked for the mindset material as well as the method. Extracted from 7 videos
and filtered hard: only things that change what you DO. Pure exhortation was
discarded, and two videos were reported as mostly noise rather than padded out.

## The five that matter most

**1. Gratification pairing** — the most novel mechanism in the set. Pure delayed
gratification sustained too long causes a crash. Pair it with a small instant
reward given **only after** the work: buy the sweets, don't eat them, do four
hours, then eat them.

**2. The 80-85% rule.** Cap daily output at ~80-85% rather than attempting 100%.
Backed by two separate self-reported burnouts on essentially the same routine
(5am, mile run, cold shower, full workday, gym, Muay Thai), both ending in forced
recovery. He gives the durations inconsistently -- "2 and 1/2 weeks" in one video,
"two months straight" in another -- so treat the number as anecdotal and the rule
as the point.

**3. Input curation, and centralisation.** Consume ALL of one source before moving
to the next; **never mix advice from multiple people on the same topic in
parallel**, because conflicting inputs mean you "retain none of it". Cut
negative-input people outright rather than moderating exposure.

*(Note the irony worth telling Zach: this knowledge base does exactly what he warns
against -- it merges 30+ of his own videos, which is how the contradictions in
`contradictions.md` surfaced. That is a feature here, but it is why they exist.)*

**4. Habit substitution AND environment removal, together.** Never just quit.
Remove the trigger object entirely (sell the console, don't shelve it) **and** fill
the exact freed time block with a named replacement.

**5. Double down.** When something is measurably working, reinvest disproportionately
into that one lane rather than diversifying. He calls it the single most repeated
word from ten millionaires he spoke to, and contrasts a billionaire who does only
microchips against the "seven income streams" myth.

## The one that links straight to the method

**Small samples lie.** A coin flipped three times can look 100% heads. Do not draw
a conclusion from a handful of tests -- run more variants before deciding something
does not work.

This is the same discipline as the 7-10 videos before expecting an outlier, and the
"don't kill a product until all four ample levers" rule. When Zach is about to call
a product dead, this is the mindset framing of a decision the method already covers.

## Goals: no deadline on the big one

The most concrete rule in the mindset material, and it cuts against the usual
advice.

**Reverse-engineer a big goal into daily and weekly actions, but never attach a
calendar deadline to the big goal itself.** Operate on "not if, but when".

He illustrates it with a POW story: the prisoner who set himself a three-month
deadline died when it passed; the one who held certainty without a date survived.
*(Worth knowing this is the Stockdale Paradox, a well-documented idea, not
something he originated -- the underlying finding is real.)*

The complement: **set deliberately EASY monthly goals you will hit regardless**,
to generate small identity-confirming wins. Deadline goals produce shame when
missed, which is the failure mode this avoids.

## Do not graph your self-worth against your revenue

Named as "a foolproof way to go rich and back to poor". When the metric drops --
and revenue oscillates permanently, which the corpus says repeatedly -- self-worth
drops with it, and performance follows.

**This is the most relevant mindset item to Zach specifically**, because his
revenue is genuinely lumpy and the dashboards make the number very visible.

## Judging what to work on
- **"Pain is progress" heuristic:** if a task is annoying and you do not want to
  do it, that is evidence it is the right one. Easy and fun is evidence it is not.
- **Timeframe relativity:** look at a current problem from a future vantage point
  -- "in four months you'll think this was stupid" -- to strip it of false
  severity.
- **The billion-dollar test:** if a billion pounds only ever covered your bills
  and bought nothing new, what would you still do daily? A sharper question than
  "ask why".

## Motivate from loss, not reward -- with his own evidence

**"Dark motivation":** anchor motivation to what happens if you do NOT succeed --
being unable to cover a family medical emergency is his example -- rather than to
a reward you are picturing.

The reason is the strongest evidence in the mindset material, because it is his
own failure: he hit $100k at 17 and **immediately lost drive**. Reward-anchored
motivation collapses the moment the reward is acquired. Loss-anchored does not.

Related technique: **compensatory drive vacuum.** Identify the specific unmet need
actually driving your ambition -- validation, power/control, or materialism -- by
laddering "why" until you cannot ask again.

## Don't force it, enable it

**Remove the friction rather than spending willpower.** Two concrete examples:
- He throws his phone across the room before bed, so the alarm physically forces
  him out of bed to silence it.
- He keeps the bedroom at 68-70F rather than the "sleep-optimal" 65F -- choosing
  the room he will actually sleep in over the theoretically correct one.

This is the same shape as habit substitution plus trigger removal, applied to
environment rather than habits.

## When a number drops, diagnose it -- do not react to it

Detach ego from the metric, then **root-cause it analytically**: "sales dropped --
is it traffic or is it conversion?" The failure mode named is panicking into
discount and CTA spam out of scarcity.

**This is the mindset half of the diagnostic funnel** in `diagnostics.md`. The
funnel tells you where to look; this tells you why people skip it.

## Two decision filters
- **Anti-sunk-cost exit:** leave once you have extracted the value you came for,
  regardless of what it cost. He leaves trips two to five days early once the
  meetings and content are banked.
- **Universal cost-benefit:** apply it to everything, and note he uses it to
  PROTECT good habits -- choosing the gym over an extra work session -- not only
  to maximise output.

## Exposure, not visualisation
The **"reps game"**: physically put yourself in the target environment until it
stops feeling foreign -- sit in cars at showrooms, try on clothes you cannot yet
afford. He cites trying a jacket on "50 times" as the point it starts to feel
deserved.

Distinct from visualisation: this is environmental exposure, not imagination.

## Examining a belief
- **Socratic sitting:** sit alone, no phone, no music, and ask "why do I believe
  this?" repeatedly until there is no answer left. A dedicated sitting, not a
  passing thought.
- **Choice of belief:** most beliefs are unfalsifiable and experience-based, so
  judge whether a belief *benefits* you rather than whether it is true.
- **Subconscious habit audit:** list EVERY autopilot behaviour, not just the one
  you want to change, and ask whether each nets a positive return.

## Setting the number
**Sum your actual monthly cost of living, then triple it** before treating that as
the income target -- the margin is the point.

## Decision rules
- **Motivate from consequence, not hope.** Frame goals around what happens if you
  do not get there. "Want" needs constant self-generation; "need" does not.
- **Region beta paradox** -- you will not act to improve an "okay" situation until
  discomfort crosses a threshold. Raise your own standard deliberately rather than
  waiting for a crisis.
- **"Once is a mistake, twice is a choice."** The rule for treating a slip
  differently from a pattern.
- **Impose a waiting period on impulsive purchases.** He waited two days before
  buying slippers.
- **Don't adopt a business model less than 5 years old** -- unless it is AI.
- **Confidence is competence.** Build reps in the specific domain rather than
  trying to feel confident abstractly.
- **Expect oscillation.** Income never moves in a straight line. The mistake is
  quitting on a downswing instead of doubling down on what worked.
- **Compete in business, not status.** Visible flexing signals a weak position.
- **Disprove a limiting belief with a real outlier** rather than arguing with it.

## Routine, where it is specific
- **Do not eat until the day's highest-priority work is done** -- so the only
  morning dopamine is tied to completed work rather than food or scrolling.
- **No caffeine until ~90 minutes after waking.**
- The **first 90 minutes** after waking set the day's output.
- **Reduce decision fatigue**: same clothes, meal prep, minimal wardrobe.
- **Strip the workspace** of anything not work-related.
- **Avoid "monk mode"** (total isolation) -- it strips serotonin regulation and
  ties your success to negativity, so you revert to isolation to earn again.
- **Read rather than consume creator content** -- books have a higher barrier to
  entry, so far fewer people act on the same knowledge.

Numbers here conflict between videos (caffeine "400 to 500 mg" in one, "200 mg" in
another) and are not reconciled by him. Treat the protocol as directional.

## A note on the source material

Twenty mindset videos were extracted. They are **overwhelmingly the same video**:
nine covering identity reduce to one causal chain -- beliefs, then identity, then
actions, then results -- restated nine ways. Only two were substantially original;
one contributed nothing new at all.

This matters for how SPZ should answer. When Zach asks about mindset, the useful
move is to give the specific rule above, not to relay the framing. The framing is
"become the person who has it", and he has heard it. The rules are the part he has
not.
