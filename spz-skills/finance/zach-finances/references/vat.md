The scheme is the load-bearing unknown. Under **standard** VAT he reclaims
input tax on purchases and pays output tax on sales. Under the **flat rate
scheme** he pays a fixed percentage of gross turnover and generally reclaims
nothing except certain capital assets — so "how much VAT can I claim back on
this" has opposite answers depending on which he is on. Do not guess.

## Structure

- Rates: 20% standard, 5% reduced, 0% zero-rated. Zero-rated is not the same
  as exempt or out of scope — zero-rated supplies still count toward turnover
  and still allow input tax recovery; exempt supplies do not.
- Registration is compulsory once taxable turnover crosses the threshold on a
  rolling 12-month basis, or if it is expected to cross it in the next 30 days
  alone. There is a separate, lower deregistration threshold. **Check both
  current figures on GOV.UK.**
- Returns are normally quarterly, due one calendar month and seven days after
  the period ends, with payment due at the same time.
- **Making Tax Digital for VAT applies to all VAT-registered businesses.**
  Records must be kept digitally and returns filed through compatible
  software, with digital links preserved end to end. A VAT figure typed by
  hand from a spreadsheet into a portal is not compliant.
- Late submission and late payment operate on a points-based penalty system,
  with interest charged separately.

## What this system can and cannot do for VAT

**It cannot prepare a return.** Specifically:

- There is no HMRC connection of any kind, so obligations, liabilities and
  previously filed returns are all invisible.
- Transaction rows carry a gross amount only. There is no VAT column, no net
  amount, and no tax code, so input tax cannot be derived from them — a
  20% assumption is wrong for anything zero-rated, exempt, or bought from a
  non-registered supplier.
- Sales are not visible at all, and output tax comes from sales.

So the honest scope is: help him find a specific payment, sanity-check a
category total, and think through a rule. Not compute a VAT liability, and
not tell him what to file.

## If he asks for a VAT figure

Say what is missing rather than approximating. The useful reply names the
three gaps — scheme unknown, no VAT breakdown on transactions, no sales data
— and offers to help him get the numbers out of whatever he actually files
with.
